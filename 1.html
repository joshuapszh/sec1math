Module 1

The Building Blocks: Prime Factorization

Before we can compare numbers, we must understand how they are built. Every whole number greater than 1 is either a Prime Number (an unbreakable block with only factors 1 and itself) or a Composite Number (a structure built by multiplying primes together). The process of breaking a composite number down into its prime blocks is called Prime Factorization. We write this using index notation (powers) to group identical blocks together.

💡 Concept: Think of Prime Factorization as finding the unique recipe or "DNA" of a number.

Module 2

Comparing Numbers: HCF & LCM

Now that we can break numbers down, we can compare them to find their Highest Common Factor (HCF) and Lowest Common Multiple (LCM). Instead of listing out all factors and multiples, we use their prime factorization "DNA" to quickly find the answers.

🛑

HCF: The Strict Bouncer

The HCF only looks at prime factors that BOTH numbers share. For those shared bases, it takes the LOWEST power. Why? Because that is the maximum amount both numbers can afford to "give".

🧲

LCM: The Greedy Collector

The LCM wants to encompass both numbers entirely. It looks at ALL unique prime factors present. For each base, it takes the HIGHEST power available to guarantee both original numbers can fit inside it perfectly.

Module 3

Roots & Powers: Squares and Cubes

Prime factorization makes finding square roots and cube roots incredibly easy without a calculator. If a number is a Perfect Square, all the powers in its prime factorization will be even (multiples of 2). To find the square root, simply halve the powers. If it's a Perfect Cube, all powers will be multiples of 3. To find the cube root, divide the powers by 3.

💡 The "Equal Groups" Rule:
• Square Root: Split the prime factors into 2 equal groups (halve the powers).
• Cube Root: Split the prime factors into 3 equal groups (divide the powers by 3).
