1 Basic Sequences & Rules

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers. To find the next terms, you must find the rule. Rules usually involve adding, subtracting, multiplying, or dividing a constant amount.

Example A (Addition)

2, 5, 8, 11, ...

$\underbrace{\quad}_{+3}\underbrace{\quad}_{+3}\underbrace{\quad}_{+3}$

Rule: Add 3 to get the next term.

Next two terms: 14, 17

Example B (Multiplication)

3, 6, 12, 24, ...

$\underbrace{\quad}_{\times2}\underbrace{\quad}_{\times2}\underbrace{\quad}_{\times2}$

Rule: Multiply by 2 to get the next term.

Next two terms: 48, 96

⚠️ Complex Rules: Some rules have two steps! For example: $1, 4, 13, 40...$ The rule is Multiply by 3 and add 1 ($\times 3 + 1$). Always test your rule on the 3rd and 4th numbers to be sure it works!

2 Special Sequences

You are expected to recognize these famous sequences on sight. Memorize their first few terms!

1, 4, 9, 16, 25...

Perfect Squares

Generated by $1^2, 2^2, 3^2, 4^2, 5^2...$

1, 8, 27, 64...

Perfect Cubes

Generated by $1^3, 2^3, 3^3, 4^3...$

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13...

Prime Numbers

Numbers divisible only by 1 and themselves.

1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13...

Fibonacci Sequence

Rule: Add the two previous terms to get the next term ($1+1=2, 1+2=3, 2+3=5$).

3 The General Term ($n$th Term)

Instead of listing 100 numbers to find the 100th term, we find an algebraic formula for the $n$th term (called $T_n$).

Formula: $T_n = a + (n - 1)d$

Use this only when the sequence adds or subtracts the same constant difference every time.

  • $a$ = The First Term
  • $d$ = The Common Difference

Example from Tutorial (Q1a)

7, 9, 11, 13, 15...

Step 1: Identify $a$ and $d$.

The first term $a = 7$.

The common difference $d = 2$ (since $9-7=2$ and $11-9=2$).

Step 2: Plug into the formula and simplify.

$$T_n = 7 + (n - 1)(2)$$

$$T_n = 7 + 2n - 2$$

$$T_n = 2n + 5$$

Step 3: Use it to find the 50th term.

Substitute $n = 50$ into your new formula.

$$T_{50} = 2(50) + 5 = 100 + 5 = \mathbf{105}$$

4 Checking if a Number is in a Sequence

Sometimes you are given the formula and asked if a specific number belongs in that sequence. Because $n$ represents a position (1st, 2nd, 3rd...), $n$ must be a positive whole number (integer).

Example from Tutorial (Q3c)

"The $n$th term of a sequence is $21 - 4n$. Explain why $-121$ is not a term of this sequence."

Step 1: Set the formula equal to the number.

$$-121 = 21 - 4n$$

Step 2: Solve for $n$.

$$4n = 21 + 121$$

$$4n = 142$$

$$n = \frac{142}{4} = 35.5$$

Step 3: Conclude based on the result.

Because $n$ is $35.5$ (which is not an integer), $-121$ cannot be a term in the sequence. You cannot have a "35.5th" term!

5 Sum of Number Patterns

To find the sum of a long sequence of numbers (like $1+2+3...+100$), you don't need a calculator. You can pair them up!

Gauss's Trick

How to find: $1 + 2 + 3 + ... + 100$

Look at the pairs from the outside in:

  • $1 + 100 = 101$
  • $2 + 99 = 101$
  • $3 + 98 = 101$

Since there are 100 numbers, there are exactly 50 pairs. Every pair adds up to 101.

Sum = $50 \times 101 = 5050$

The Shortcut Formula:

$$\text{Sum of } 1+2+...+n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$$

Example: For $1$ to $50$, just calculate $(50 \times 51) / 2 = 1275$.

6 Real-World Contexts (Figures & Shapes)

Patterns often appear visually as matchsticks, tiles, or tables. Treat the "Figure Number" as $n$, and count the objects to find the sequence terms.

Example: Banquet Tables

"A banquet room has tables. 1 table sits 6 people. 2 tables joined together sit 10 people. 3 tables joined together sit 14 people. Find a formula for $p$ people at $n$ tables."

Step 1: Write out the sequence.

Number of tables ($n$): 1, 2, 3...

Number of people ($p$): 6, 10, 14...

Step 2: Identify $a$ and $d$.

First term $a = 6$. Common difference $d = 4$ (adding a table adds 4 people).

Step 3: Create the formula.

$$p = 6 + (n - 1)(4)$$

$$p = 6 + 4n - 4$$

$$p = 4n + 2$$

Follow-up: How many tables for 50 people?

$$50 = 4n + 2$$

$$48 = 4n$$

$$n = 12 \text{ tables}$$
