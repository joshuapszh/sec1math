1 The Cartesian Coordinate System

The Cartesian plane is formed by two perpendicular lines that intersect at the Origin $(0,0)$. The horizontal line is the $x$-axis and the vertical line is the $y$-axis.

Ordered Pairs $(x, y)$

The order is strictly (horizontal distance, vertical distance).

Plotting $A(2, 3)$

Start at the origin. Move 2 units right (the x-coordinate), then 3 units up (the y-coordinate).

Plotting $C(-4, -2)$

Start at the origin. Move 4 units left, then 2 units down.

⚠️ Exam Fact: The equation of the $x$-axis itself is $y = 0$, and the equation of the $y$-axis is $x = 0$.

2 Linear Functions (Inputs & Outputs)

A function is a relationship where every input ($x$) gives exactly one output ($y$). Evaluating a function just means substituting a number into the equation.

Textbook Example: $y = 3x - 2$

(a) Find $y$ when $x = 4$

$$y = 3(4) - 2$$

$$y = 12 - 2 = \mathbf{10}$$

(b) Find $x$ when $y = -1$

Substitute $-1$ for $y$ and use algebra to solve for $x$:

$$-1 = 3x - 2$$

$$-1 + 2 = 3x$$

$$1 = 3x \implies x = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}}$$

3 Graphing $y = mx + c$

Every straight line can be written as $y = mx + c$, where $m$ is the gradient and $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

The Intercepts

  • $y$-intercept ($c$): Where the line crosses the $y$-axis. The $x$-coordinate is always $0$.
  • $x$-intercept: Where the line crosses the $x$-axis. The $y$-coordinate is always $0$.

Drawing the Line

To draw a straight line accurately, you need a minimum of 3 points. Create a table of values (e.g., choose $x = 0, 2, 4$), calculate the corresponding $y$ values, plot the coordinates, and connect them with a ruler.

Identify $m$ and $c$:

For $y = -\frac{1}{2}x - 10$ :

Gradient ($m$) = $-\frac{1}{2}$, $y$-intercept ($c$) = $-10$

4 Finding the Gradient (Slope)

Gradient measures steepness. The formula is: Gradient = $\frac{\text{Vertical Change (Rise)}}{\text{Horizontal Change (Run)}}$

↗️

Positive Gradient

Slopes upwards to the right. ($m > 0$)

↘️

Negative Gradient

Slopes downwards to the right. ($m < 0$)

Textbook Example: Points $(0,0)$ and $(3,-4)$

Step 1: Find the Run (horizontal)

From $x=0$ to $x=3$, the run is $3$ units.

Step 2: Find the Rise (vertical)

From $y=0$ to $y=-4$, the rise is $-4$ units (it went down!).

Step 3: Calculate

$$\text{Gradient} = \frac{-4}{3} = -1\frac{1}{3}$$

5 Horizontal and Vertical Lines

Lines that are perfectly straight up-and-down or left-to-right have very simple equations and unique gradients.

Horizontal Lines (Left & Right)

Equation format: $y = c$

Passes through the y-axis at $c$.

Gradient = 0

(Because rise is 0, and 0 / run = 0)

Vertical Lines (Up & Down)

Equation format: $x = c$

Passes through the x-axis at $c$.

Gradient = Undefined

(Because run is 0, and you cannot divide by 0)

6 Conversion Graphs

Conversion graphs are linear graphs used to convert one unit or quantity into another (like currencies or distances). The axes represent the two different units.

Textbook Example: Miles to Kilometres

Imagine a graph where the x-axis is Miles and the y-axis is Kilometres.

Converting 8 miles to km:

Find $8$ on the Miles (x) axis. Draw a straight line UP until you hit the graph line. Then draw a straight line LEFT to read the Kilometres (y) axis. The reading is approx $13\text{ km}$.

Converting 24 kilometres to miles:

Find $24$ on the Kilometres (y) axis. Draw a straight line RIGHT until you hit the graph. Then draw a straight line DOWN to read the Miles (x) axis. The reading is exactly $15\text{ miles}$.

7 Distance-Time Graphs

These graphs show a journey. Distance from the start is on the vertical axis ($y$), and Time is on the horizontal axis ($x$).

🌟 THE GOLDEN RULE: In a distance-time graph, the gradient of the line represents the SPEED of the object.

Mrs Lee's Journey to the Bank

Line segment sloping up (e.g., $O$ to $A$):

She is travelling away from home. Gradient = Speed. She traveled $9\text{ km}$ in $10\text{ mins}$ ($\frac{1}{6}\text{ hour}$).
Speed = $\frac{9}{(1/6)} = \mathbf{54\text{ km/h}}$.

Horizontal line segment (e.g., $A$ to $B$):

The distance isn't changing as time passes. She is stationary (at the bank). The gradient is $0$, meaning speed is $0\text{ km/h}$. She stayed there from $t=10$ to $t=30$ ($20\text{ minutes}$).

Line segment sloping down (e.g., $D$ to $E$):

She is travelling back towards home (distance is decreasing back to $0$).

Calculating Average Speed for the Whole Journey:

$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance Travelled}}{\text{Total Time Taken}}$$

Note: Total distance includes the trip *there* and the trip *back*. If she went $9\text{km}$ to the bank and $9\text{km}$ back, total distance is $18\text{km}$!
