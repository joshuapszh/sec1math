Ch 1 Factors and Multiples
Prime Numbers

A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself.

Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 …
Prime Factorisation

Process of breaking up a composite number into its prime factors using repeated division.

Example — Prime factorisation of 1176
$$1176 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 7 \times 7 = 2^3 \times 3 \times 7^2 \quad \text{(Index Notation)}$$
Highest Common Factor (H.C.F)

The H.C.F of two (or more) numbers is the largest number that divides evenly into all the numbers.

To find H.C.F in index form: compare the factors and take the Lower Power.
Example

Find the H.C.F of $\ 2 \times 3^2 \times 5\ $ and $\ 2^2 \times 3^3 \times 7 \times 13$

$2 \times 3^2 \times 5$
$2^2 \times 3^3 \times 7 \times 13$
$$\text{HCF} = 2 \times 3^2 = 18$$
Lowest Common Multiple (L.C.M)

The L.C.M of two (or more) numbers is the smallest number that is divisible by all the numbers.

To find L.C.M in index form: compare the factors and take the Higher Power.
Example
$2 \times 3^2 \times 5^2 \times 11$
$2^3 \times 3 \times 5^3$
$$\text{LCM} = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5^3 \times 11$$

Substitution
Example — given $x=5,\ y=-2$, evaluate $\dfrac{x}{2x+y} - \dfrac{y}{x}$
$$\frac{x}{2x+y} - \frac{y}{x} = \frac{5}{2(5)+(-2)} - \frac{-2}{5} = \frac{5}{8} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{25}{40}+\frac{16}{40} = \frac{41}{40}$$

Equations Involving Algebraic Fractions

Multiply every term by the LCM of all denominators to clear fractions.

Example — Solve: $\dfrac{2p+1}{6} - \dfrac{6-5p}{5} = \dfrac{12p-15}{10}$

Multiply each term by 30 (LCM of 6, 5 and 10)

$$\left(\frac{2p+1}{6}\right)\times30 - \left(\frac{6-5p}{5}\right)\times30 = \left(\frac{12p-15}{10}\right)\times30$$
$5(2p+1) - 6(6-5p) = 3(12p-15)$
$10p + 5 - 36 + 30p = 36p - 45$
$40p - 31 = 36p - 45$
$4p = -14$
$$p = \frac{7}{2}$$

Cartesian Plane — Coordinates
Cartesian plane — A(2, 5) and B(−5, 3)
Gradient

The gradient determines the slope of a line.

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$$
Change in $y = 11-7 = 4$, Change in $x = 3-1 = 2$, Gradient $= 4 \div 2 = 2$
Different Gradients
Lines through the origin with different gradients
Equation of a Straight Line
$$y = mx + c \qquad (m = \text{gradient},\quad c = \text{y-intercept})$$
Example — from the graph of A(1,7) and B(3,11)
$m = 4 \div 2 = 2$
$c = y\text{-intercept} = 5$
$$\therefore\quad y = 2x + 5$$

Ch 11 Ratio and Proportion
What is a Ratio?

A ratio is a comparison of two quantities in the same units.

Example
$3\ \text{m} : 20\ \text{cm} = 300\ \text{cm} : 20\ \text{cm} = 15 : 1$
$15 : 1 = \dfrac{15}{1}$
Equivalent Ratios
Example — $\dfrac{1}{2} : 3 = \dfrac{2}{3} x : 5$
$$\frac{1}{2} \div 3 \times 5 = \frac{2}{3}x \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{5}{6} = \frac{2}{3}x \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{5}{6} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{4}$$
Combining Ratios (Repeated Identity Concept)
Example — $a:b = 2:3$ and $b:c = 4:5$, find $a:b:c$
$a:b$$b:c$
$= 2:3$$= 4:5$
$= 8:12$ (×4)$= 12:15$ (×3)
$$a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15$$
