1 The Basics & Unknowns on Both Sides

The golden rule of algebra: Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other. The goal is to isolate $x$ on one side of the equal sign.

Example from Tutorial (Q1g)

$$15x - 8 = 4 + 9x$$

Step 1: Group the $x$ terms together.

Subtract $9x$ from both sides to move it to the left.

$$15x - 9x - 8 = 4$$

$$6x - 8 = 4$$

Step 2: Group the numbers together.

Add $8$ to both sides to move the constant to the right.

$$6x = 4 + 8$$

$$6x = 12$$

Step 3: Isolate $x$.

Divide both sides by 6.

$$x = 2$$

⚠️ Exam Tip: When moving terms across the equal sign, always remember to change their sign (positive becomes negative, negative becomes positive).

2 Expanding Brackets

Before you can move terms around, you must "unlock" them from their brackets using the distributive property.

Example from Tutorial (Q3a)

$$5(x + 2) - 3(x - 1) = 12$$

Step 1: Expand the brackets carefully.

Multiply the number outside by everything inside. Watch the negative signs!

$$5(x) + 5(2) - 3(x) - 3(-1) = 12$$

$$5x + 10 - 3x + 3 = 12$$

Step 2: Simplify by combining like terms.

$$(5x - 3x) + (10 + 3) = 12$$

$$2x + 13 = 12$$

Step 3: Solve for $x$.

$$2x = 12 - 13$$

$$2x = -1$$

$$x = -\frac{1}{2} \text{ or } -0.5$$

🚨 THE MOST COMMON EXAM MISTAKE: In the expression $-3(x - 1)$, many students write $-3x - 3$. It MUST be $-3x + 3$ because a negative times a negative is a positive. Double-check this in your exams!

3 Equations with Fractions

Fractions look intimidating, but you can destroy them immediately by multiplying the entire equation by the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of all denominators.

Example from Tutorial (Q6a)

$$\frac{x}{4} + \frac{x-2}{8} = 5$$

Step 1: Find the LCM of the denominators.

The denominators are 4 and 8. The LCM is 8.

Step 2: Multiply EVERY term by the LCM to clear fractions.

$$8 \cdot \left(\frac{x}{4}\right) + 8 \cdot \left(\frac{x-2}{8}\right) = 8 \cdot (5)$$

Simplify the fractions:

$$2(x) + 1(x - 2) = 40$$

Step 3: Expand and solve.

$$2x + x - 2 = 40$$

$$3x - 2 = 40$$

$$3x = 42$$

$$x = 14$$

💡 Pro-Tip for Single Fractions (Cross-Multiplication): If you have one fraction equal to another fraction like $\frac{2x-1}{3} = \frac{x+2}{4}$ (Q4d), you can skip the LCM and just cross-multiply! $4(2x-1) = 3(x+2)$.

4 Formulas and Substitution

Sometimes you aren't solving for $x$, but substituting known values into a physical or mathematical formula to find an unknown.

Example from Tutorial (Q8b)

$$E = \frac{W}{W+X}$$

Find the value of $X$ when $E = \frac{5}{8}$ and $W = 25$.

Step 1: Substitute the given numbers into the formula.

$$\frac{5}{8} = \frac{25}{25+X}$$

Step 2: Cross-multiply to remove fractions.

$$5(25 + X) = 8 \cdot 25$$

$$125 + 5X = 200$$

Step 3: Solve for $X$.

$$5X = 200 - 125$$

$$5X = 75$$

$$X = 15$$

5 Real-World Word Problems

Word problems test your ability to translate English into Algebra. Define your unknown variable clearly first!

Example from Tutorial (Q41a) - Age Problem

"Jonathan is 15 years older than his brother Jeremy. Six years ago, Jonathan was six times as old as Jeremy. Find their present ages."

Step 1: Define variables for the present day.

Let Jeremy's current age be $x$.

Therefore, Jonathan's current age is $x + 15$.

Step 2: Express their ages "Six years ago".

Jeremy's age then: $x - 6$

Jonathan's age then: $(x + 15) - 6 = x + 9$

Step 3: Form the equation using the clue.

"Jonathan was six times as old as Jeremy."

$$x + 9 = 6(x - 6)$$

Step 4: Solve.

$$x + 9 = 6x - 36$$

$$9 + 36 = 6x - x$$

$$45 = 5x$$

$$x = 9$$

Jeremy is 9 years old. Jonathan is 24 years old.

📝 Vocab Cheatsheet:
• Consecutive integers: $x, x+1, x+2$
• Consecutive even/odd integers: $x, x+2, x+4$
• Exceeds by 5: $x + 5$

6 Geometry Applications

Algebra is frequently mixed with geometry. You must know formulas for Perimeter (sum of all outer sides) and Area.

Example from Tutorial (Q60)

"Rectangle A has sides $(x-6)$ and $(x+1)$. Rectangle B has sides $(x-2)$ and $(x-1)$. The perimeter of Rectangle A is $\frac{9}{11}$ of the perimeter of Rectangle B. Form an equation and solve for $x$."

Step 1: Write expressions for the perimeters.

Perimeter = 2(length) + 2(width)

Perimeter A = $2(x-6) + 2(x+1) = 2x - 12 + 2x + 2 = \mathbf{4x - 10}$

Perimeter B = $2(x-2) + 2(x-1) = 2x - 4 + 2x - 2 = \mathbf{4x - 6}$

Step 2: Form the equation.

$$4x - 10 = \frac{9}{11}(4x - 6)$$

Step 3: Clear fractions and solve.

Multiply both sides by 11:

$$11(4x - 10) = 9(4x - 6)$$

$$44x - 110 = 36x - 54$$

$$44x - 36x = 110 - 54$$

$$8x = 56$$

$$x = 7$$
